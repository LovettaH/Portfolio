Case Study 01 · iOGYM 2024–Now

Fixing the wrong problem — and finding the right one

When everyone assumed users left because sensors were inaccurate, user research revealed a different culprit entirely: the app was just too troublesome to use.

RoleUX Research · Product Manager
MethodsUser interviews · Flow analysis
OutcomeRedirected dev priorities

The problem

Assumed cause

Sensor data was inaccurate — users couldn't trust the equipment readings, so they stopped using the app.

Real cause (discovered)

The app flow was too cumbersome. Users had to open app → scan QR → select exercise → go rest → manually adjust reps and sets every single time.

"I have to open the app, scan, select exercise, go to rest and adjust my figures... it's just too much." — gym user interview

Research process

Define assumptions
→
Interview gym coaches + users
→
Map user flow pain points
→
Identify real churn driver

[ Insert: interview notes / affinity map ]

Key insights

01

Friction in the workout flow was the #1 churn driver — not data accuracy

02

Users wanted the app to remember them — previous weights, last reps

03

Encouragement and progress feedback increased motivation to return

Design decisions

Short term fix

Auto-remember previous weight inputs. Add encouragement messages. Show "you lifted more than last time" feedback after each session.

Future vision — NFC flow

Phone rack with NFC: user places phone → workout starts automatically → adjust reps during rest. Zero friction entry point.

[ Insert: NFC flow diagram vs current QR flow ]

[ Insert: Figma screens — encouragement UI, weight memory feature ]

Reflection

The biggest lesson was learning to separate the assumed problem from the real one. Stakeholders were convinced the sensors were the issue — but going directly to users revealed the friction was in the journey, not the data. Working within a donor-driven product environment also taught me how to advocate for user needs within real organisational constraints.

Case Study 02 · iOGYM B2B Research

What keeps gym members coming back?

iOGYM sells to gyms, not individual users. So the real question isn't "why do users churn" — it's "what data can help gyms prevent it before it happens?"

RoleProduct Research · Strategy
MethodsQuestionnaire · Branding framework
StatusIn progress

Why this research matters

iOGYM's real customer is the gym, not the individual member. But our product was designed around individual tracking. This misalignment meant gyms couldn't see the value — because we weren't solving their actual problem: member retention.

Research questions

For gym members

What motivates you to keep coming back? Is it the trainer, your friends, the environment, the classes?

For gyms

What data would help you identify members at risk of cancelling — before they actually do?

[ Insert: questionnaire results / survey data from branding course ]

Early insights

01

Social circle and workout buddies are the strongest long-term retention driver

02

Different member types need different interventions — one size doesn't fit all

03

Gyms need early warning signals, not just historical usage data

Proposed product direction

Member tagging system

Tag members by type: needs social connection, needs trainer guidance, needs class variety, needs nutrition support.

Early warning dashboard

Show gyms which members haven't visited recently, and suggest what type of outreach fits their profile.

[ Insert: proposed dashboard wireframe ]

Reflection

This research reframed the entire product strategy — from "track individual exercise data accurately" to "help gyms understand and retain their members." The shift from B2C thinking to genuine B2B product thinking was the most important strategic insight from two years at iOGYM.

Visual Design · iOGYM 2024–Now

Making complex fitness data feel simple

Designing weekly and monthly training reports for a dark-mode fitness app — translating multi-dimensional performance data into clean, motivating visuals.

RoleUX · Visual Design
ToolsFigma
ContextIoT fitness app, dark mode

Design principles

01

Non-judgmental — data shows progress without making users feel bad about gaps

02

Dark mode first — gym environment, low light, glare reduction

03

Zero border radius — clean, sharp, serious. Inspired by iPhone Health app patterns

Design decision

Monthly report

[ Insert: monthly report Figma screens ]

What it shows

Month-over-month comparison, consistency score, personal records, progress encouragement

Design decision

Encouragement language over raw numbers — celebrates consistency and effort, not just peak performance

Reflection

The challenge was designing for data that was sometimes unreliable — sensors weren't always accurate, so the UI needed to present numbers with appropriate context rather than false precision. This taught me that good data visualization isn't just about aesthetics: it's about honest communication with the user, especially when the underlying data has limitations.
